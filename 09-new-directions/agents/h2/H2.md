## 1. Idea 1: the diagonal-free structure of the mixed moment

### 1.1 The mixed diagonal is a short cubic character sum [P; N]

Δ_{12} = Σ_k V Σ_n |βw|²(n) ξ(n) 1_{(n,k)=1}, with ξ = χ_{P1}^{2}χ_{P2}^{-2} nonprincipal mod P1P2.

Proof outline:
1. Write μ²(n) = Σ_{a²|n} μ(a), and expand the coprimality by Möbius.
2. For N(a)² ≤ N/L^{2+δ}, the smooth Hecke sum over m with conductor ≤ L² is negligible.
3. For larger a, use min(N/N(a)², L).

Result: **|Δ_{12}| ≪ K(NL)^{1/2}Z^ε.** This is below the (H2) target KN/L iff N ≥ L³ ⟺ ℓ ≤ M − ½. That holds at the (H2) optimum
(0.288 ≤ 0.407).

Numerically, Δ/√(G11G22) = 0.0186 at N0 = 2·10⁴ and 0.0131 at 4·10⁴, i.e. ∝ N^{−1/2}, independent of L0. In the numerics N < L²,
so Δ is not negligible there and must be subtracted; this is the source of all apparent structure in §4.1.

### 1.2 No inequality of the form |O12| ≲ (O11·O22)^{1/2} exists [P, with numerical certificate]

Set Γ = V^*V (the n-space Gram of the K′ rows), D = the exact diagonal weights, and Q(x,y) = ⟨Vx,Vy⟩ − ⟨x,Dy⟩, the off-diagonal
form. Beyond the wall, K′ < N, so rank Γ ≤ K′ and **Q = −D ≈ −K·I on ker Γ, which has dimension ≥ N − K′**. So Q is indefinite,
with negative mass comparable to the full diagonal.

**Lemma 1.** Let Π be the D-orthogonal projection onto ker V. If ‖Πx₁‖²_D ≥ ½‖x₁‖²_D and dim ker V ≥ 2, there is x₂ = x₁ + z, z ∈ ker V, with:
- ⟨x₁,Dx₂⟩ = 0 and ‖x₂‖_D = ‖x₁‖_D;
- hence O22 = O11 and Δ12 = 0, but |O12| = G11 = Δ11 + O11.

Proof: take z = −tΠx₁ + w with t = ‖x₁‖²_D/‖Πx₁‖²_D and ‖w‖²_D = (2 − t)‖x₁‖²_D.

Consequence: no bound |O12| ≤ F(O11, O22, Δ12, |x₁|, |x₂|) with F(a,a,0,·,·) < a + Δ11 exists. Generic vectors have
‖Πx‖² ≈ (1 − K′/N)‖x‖², so the hypothesis holds whenever N ≥ 2K′, i.e. everywhere beyond the wall.

Numerics (`h2_kernel.py`):
- The rank deficiency of Γ is exactly N − K′ (2522 at N0 = 10⁴).
- Q has 87% / 91% negative eigenvalues (N0 = 3·10³ / 10⁴). Their median is −70.9 / −141.8, against mean diagonal 69.8 / 137.2.
- Mark vectors put a mass fraction 0.838 / 0.892 of their norm in ker Γ, against the prediction 1 − K′/N = 0.836 / 0.896.
- Alternating projections, started from an actual mark column x₁ = βwχ_P^{-2}, produce a **flat** x₂ (|x₂(n)| = |x₁(n)| to 2·10⁻¹³ rms).
  It has the same row sums, zero mixed diagonal and O22 = O11, yet |O12| = G11.
  - N0 = 3·10³: |O12| = 5.8·√(O11O22).
  - N0 = 10⁴: x₁ itself is almost exactly diagonal (O11/Δ11 = 0.8%), and x₂ is flat to 10⁻¹⁶ rms with |Δ12| = 2·10⁻¹².
    Yet |O12| = G11 = 1.008·Δ11 = **124·√(O11O22)** (`runs/kernel_N10000.out`).
- In the real data, |O12|² ≤ O11⁺O22⁺ fails for **79–99% of pairs** (`summary.txt`); the 2×2 off-diagonal forms are indefinite in the same fraction.

The only positive form available is Γ itself, so Cauchy–Schwarz with full diagonals is the only inequality of this type. A
refinement needs Γ ≥ (1−η)D on span{x_P}, i.e. the marks avoid ker Γ. That is (H2) itself.

**Equivalent formulations [P].**
- W uniform in the marks ⟺ the uncertainty principle ‖Π_V Π_X‖² ≲ (K/N)Z^ε. Here Π_V projects onto span{ψ_k} ⊂ C^N and Π_X onto span{βχ_P^{-2}}.
- (H2) in mean square (§2.4) ⟺ second-order freeness of the two projections:
  N⁴[tr(Π_VΠ_XΠ_VΠ_X) − Σ_P (u_P^*Π_Vu_P)²] ≪ K²N², with u_P = x_P/‖x_P‖.
- For free projections the left side is ≈ L′²KN², so this holds iff L′² ≤ K (the random-model threshold ℓ ≤ M/2).

### 1.3 The Poisson involution with exact smooth weights: no contraction [P]

For squarefree coprime n1, n2 and m = n1n2, Poisson in k gives exactly

  Σ_k W(k/K)(k/m) = (K/m) Σ_{h∈Z} τ_m(h) Ŵ(hK/m),

`h2_poisson_Z.py`, check A: matches to 10⁻⁶. In Z[ω] it is the same identity with the 2-dimensional transform of the radial weight.

- The h = 0 term exists only for n1 = n2. Hence both diagonals of the mixed moment are the same character sum Σ_n ξ(n), and both are small.
- **The "dual diagonal" with exact weights is O(τ(n)) per column.** For n² > K,

  Σ_{h≠0}(…) = −Ŵ(0)Σ_{d|n, d>K}μ(d)K/d + (tails from d < K),

  because Σ_{h∈Z}Ŵ(hK/d) = 0 for d > K (check B: |E(n)| ≤ 0.47 for all 31 columns tested, against N = 300 under crude bookkeeping).
- Replacing the oscillating weight Ŵ(hK/n1n2) by a positive bump on |h| ≤ N²/K creates two fake terms: a dual diagonal (K/N)·HN = N²,
  and a matching fake dual off-diagonal of size −N². Spectrally [H, Marchenko–Pastur], the positive-weight dual of Γ − D has bulk eigenvalues near +K,
  while the true operator has −K on its (N − K′)-dimensional kernel [N, §1.2]. **This is the source of the earlier inconsistent identity.**
- With exact weights the involution Φ maps O12(K) to (K/N)·O*12(N²/K), and Φ∘Φ = id (Fourier inversion). A linear involution has
  spectrum {±1}, so iterating gives no contraction and no fixed point. The only independent input is a bound on the dual. With
  absolute values on the weight it returns |O12| ≤ (K/N)(H+N)N = N², the large sieve.

## 2. Idea 2: the cocycle

### 2.1 Exact identities [P]

(a) **The mark is the operator U_P.** From β(Pn) = β(P)β(n)χ_P(n)⁴ and χ⁴ = χ^{-2}:

  D_P(k) = conj β(P)·ψ_k(P)·Θ_k^{(P)},  with Θ_k^{(P)} = Σ_{m~NL, P∥m} β(m)ψ_k(m).

(b) **The cross term is a twisted correlation of two pieces of one theta sum.**

  G_{P1P2} = β(P1)conj β(P2)·Σ_k V ψ_k(P1P2)·conj Θ_k^{(P1)}·Θ_k^{(P2)}.

These are the P1- and P2-progression pieces of the same unmarked theta sum at length NL. The k-sum is twisted by the quadratic
character (P1P2/k)₂.

(c) **Single-mark form.** x_{P1} = y·χ_{P2}^{2} and x_{P2} = y·χ_{P1}^{2}, where y = βχ_{P1P2}^{-2} is the single (product) mark of
conductor (P1P2)². So G12 is a **mixed** moment of the single mark P1P2 between two cubic twists. It is not an energy. By
polarization it is a difference of energies of 2-slot marks y(χ_{P2}² ± λχ_{P1}²), each beyond its own wall.

In the Möbius dual (edge-theta, Lemma M) it is Σ_h ξ12(h)|M_h|²: one character of conductor P1P2 applied to the **unmarked** density.
That is a twisted *first* moment, not a second moment.

(d) **Conjugation symmetry** (checked: |β(n̄) − conj β(n)| ≤ 5·10⁻¹⁵ on all 1416 columns). β(n̄) = conj β(n), so
D_{P̄}(k̄) = conj D_P(k) and G_{P,P̄} = conj Σ_k V D_P(k)D_P(k̄). The k and k̄ terms coincide, which doubles the variance of
conjugate-pair entries. Observed: θ 1.72 / 1.81 (N0 = 2·10⁴ / 4·10⁴) against random columns 1.00 / 0.98. This is harmless, since it
concerns only O(L′) of the L′² pairs.

### 2.2 Pair-averaging does not restore a family at the edge [B]

E(c) = c^H G c is a **rank-one** functional of the pair matrix cc^H:
- **Coherent c:** E(c) is the K-member quadratic family acting on y = βX at length N, beyond its edge by N/K.
- **Incoherent (label-averaged) c:** E is Σ|c_P|²G_PP. That is diagonal in P, gives (HLS) and has no cross terms (mark-design, L3).

No intermediate choice presents KL² orthogonal objects.

Concretely, in the dual the pair characters {ξ12} number L′², have conductor L², and act on h ~ H = Z^{1−w}L³ > L⁴. The large sieve
over them gives:

  Σ_pairs |Σ_h ξ12(h)|M_h|²|² ≤ (H + L⁴)Σ_h|M_h|⁴ ≈ H²Z²,

i.e. rms |T(P1,P2)| ≈ HZ/L, against the need Z². This fails by F_m = H/(ZL) = L²Z^{−w}, exactly the distance to the wall.

As a family of characters on pairs (n1,n2), the triple family (k,P1,P2) has KL² members on an N²-dimensional space. It is beyond its
edge by N²/(KL²) = K³/Z² = Z^{3M−2} (Z^{0.72} at M = 0.907).

### 2.3 Reflected cocycle and a cubic large sieve [H]; refuted numerically [N]

Twisted multiplicativity of Gauss sums puts χ_P(k)^{∓4} = (k/P)₃^{±1} into the root number of θ⊗ψ_kχ_P^{-2}. After reflection, the
cross term carries the cubic character ξ12(k) of conductor P1P2 in the **family** variable.

Toy model: if the reflected amplitudes F_P(k) were independent of P, Heath-Brown's cubic large sieve would give
E(c) ≤ max|F|²·(K + L′ + (KL′)^{2/3})‖c‖². That is (H2) strength iff L′² ≤ K, the same threshold as the random model.

Test: twist column P at row k by χ_k(P)^aχ_P(k)^b for all 36 pairs (a,b). By sextic reciprocity this equals χ_P(k)^{a+b}𝔯^a.
- The 3 trivial combinations reproduce the untwisted rms (which includes Δ).
- The 3 combinations with twist 𝔯 (sign mod 4) keep part of Δ.
- **All 30 genuine twists give the same rms**: 0.0256 (N0 = 2·10⁴) and 0.0181 (4·10⁴) for both θ and random columns.

Moreover √(0.0256² + 0.0186²) = 0.0316 ≈ 0.0308, the untwisted value. So there is no hidden cocycle phase: D_{P1}D̄_{P2}(k)
correlates with no character of k. The full spectrum over all characters mod P1P2 (§4.4) confirms this. The reflected amplitudes
depend on P through χ_P(n)² at length ZL > N, which is further beyond the edge. **Dead.**

### 2.4 A weaker sufficient condition: (H2) in mean square [P]

T(c) ≤ ‖c‖²·‖G − diag G‖_op ≤ ‖c‖²·‖G − diag G‖_F, so

  **(H2-ms)** Σ_{P1≠P2} |G_{P1P2}|² ≪ (KN)² Z^ε

suffices for 19/22. (H2-ms) with mean saving L^{−θ₂} gives the same Hoff = 1 − θ₂ as per-pair (H2).

The random model satisfies (H2-ms) iff L′² ≤ K: margin Z^{0.19} at the optimum.

However, (H2-ms) is a 4th moment of the hybrid family, i.e. second-order freeness (§1.2) [H]:
- any orthogonality expansion must remove the P-diagonal Σ_P G_PP² ≈ L′(KN)²;
- that needs (H1) *asymptotically* with mean relative error ≤ 1/L′;
- the generic relative error N/(KL) of the hybrid family meets this iff M + ℓ ≤ 1. **The wall reappears as a precision requirement.**

## 3. Idea 3: the a priori zero-free half-plane β* ≤ 7/8 [B]

**Primal.** By wall-spectral (∗), βψ_kχ_P^{-2} = μ·G𝔯·conj γ₁(·;k³P⁴). μ·conj γ₁ is twisted-multiplicative and Σ βψχn^{-s} is the
θ-twist L-function, which has no Euler product. β* has nothing to act on.

**Dual (Lemma M).** M_h = Σ_n μḠ(h/n)₆V is a partial sum of 1/L(s,ψ_h), with ψ_h a Hecke character mod 12h. Re s > β* zero-free
gives 1/L ≪ (H(1+|t|))^ε on Re s ≥ β* + ε, hence |M_h| ≪ Z^{β*+ε} and |T(P1,P2) − C| ≪ H·Z^{2β*+ε}.

Lemma M needs Z^{2+ε} for H up to ZL³. Any pointwise bound |M_h| ≤ Z^σ therefore needs σ ≤ (1 − 3ℓ)/2:
- that is 0.068 at the (H2) geometry and 0.194 at the 27/31 geometry, both < ½;
- even GRH falls short by a factor HZ^{-1} = Z^{0.67} at the bottom of the range H = Z^{1−w}L³, and β* = 7/8 by Z^{1.42}.

**Cross-term refinement** (the one place β* could act on the twist). On Re s = σ > β*, write 1/L(s,ψ_h) = (Dirichlet polynomial of
length Y) + O(Y^{β*−σ+ε}).
- For Y ≤ H^{1/2}/L, the short×short part is negligible: ψ_{P1}ψ̄_{P2}(h)(h/n1)₆conj(h/n2)₆ is nonprincipal of conductor ≤ L²Y² < H.
- The tails give |T − C| ≪ Z^{2σ}·H·Y^{−(σ−β*)}.
- The Perron cost Z^{2σ} grows faster than the truncation saves (d/dσ: 2 − y > 0 with Y = Z^y, y < 1), so the optimum is σ = β*.
  There is no gain over the pointwise bound.

**Saving against L^{−0.93}: none.** θ₂ = 0 for every β* ∈ [½, 1). At best (GRH) the route reproduces the hybrid large sieve
(edge-theta O2).

## 5. What a proof of (H2) would have to supply

- Every cancellation in G_{P1P2} is random-matrix cancellation: rms O ≈ 1.2·rows^{-1/2}, identical for θ and random columns. It is
  not generated by any arithmetic structure in (P1,P2) or in k.
- So a proof cannot "identify a main term and cancel it". It must prove a **freeness / uncertainty statement** between the
  quadratic-row space and the cubic mark space (§1.2). The weakest sufficient form is **(H2-ms)**, a 4th moment of the hybrid family.
- Inputs that do *not* suffice, with proofs here:
  - positivity and off-diagonal Cauchy–Schwarz (§1.2);
  - Poisson involutions with exact weights (§1.3);
  - pair-averaging and large sieves over pairs (§2.2);
  - cocycle phases and cubic large sieves (§2.3);
  - zero-free regions (§3).
