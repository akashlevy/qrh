Independent adversarial audit of the kappa-bootstrap, 9 October 2026

Audited proposal:
  research_audit/plain_moment_extension.txt
  research_audit/bootstrap_certificate.py

Source manuscript:
  formalize_11_12/papers/paper78.tex

Verdict
-------
I found no fatal mathematical obstruction to the proposed first bootstrap
step, relative to the source manuscript's analytic theorems. The extension
of its fourth-moment lemma has a concrete proof by modifying two numerical
inequalities, not an additional conjectural mean-value input. The first
stage's exact certificate was independently rerun and passed. Its negative
control rejected the overstrong target.

This is an audit of a mathematical amendment and its exponent certificate.
It is not a fresh verification of every theorem in the 16,000-line source,
and it is not Lean kernel verification of the new theorem. The low-side
Gram split and its missing pi-pairing estimate are not used in this route.

1. Range extension of Lemma plain
--------------------------------
I searched every occurrence of kappa and the potentially consequential
constants 3/4, 7/2, 2M/9, M/21, M/20 in the complete printed proof, lines
12531--14986. The uses identified in the proposed amendment are complete.

The source's prime bound uses logarithmic control at
  s_kappa=(1+kappa)/2 >= beta_*.
For kappa>=13/18, s_kappa>=31/36. The logarithmic-control lemma is uniform
on a in [1/2,1], hence remains applicable. The fixed positive contour
displacement e, rather than a positive gap between s_kappa and beta_*,
gives the zero-free contour. Equality beta_*=s_kappa causes no problem.
Expanding the finitely many fixed slot characters preserves the class of
finite-order Hecke characters to which the global bound applies.

At the terminal width floor, the only required error is kappa*z<=M/6.
The auxiliary estimate z<=2M/9 changes to z<=3M/13, but no later
argument needs the sharper former estimate.

In the positive-slot centered comparison,
  (6*kappa-1) z <= M-A < M/6.
Since 6*(13/18)-1=10/3, this implies z<M/20. That is exactly the
bound used in the two subsequent estimates:
  A_comp <= 23M/30+xi,
  A_comp+(6*kappa-1)z <= 14M/15+xi.
Both required distances to the boundary are at least M/15 before xi,
so the existing choice xi<=rho/30 survives unchanged. Also A-z>2L,
where L=M/4, by a large margin.

The remaining uses need only kappa>0, kappa<=1, and
0<=6*kappa-1<=5. In particular, removing a slot of length d reduces
the affine defect by 6*kappa*d at squared cost kappa*d, yielding the
same defect/6 charge and the same one-slot overshoot. The aggregate
Lipschitz constant remains bounded by |Delta M|+|Delta A|+5|Delta z|.
The mesh/slot-count quantifier ordering and induction depth therefore
do not acquire a dependence on a gap kappa-3/4.

2. The modified selected-row count
---------------------------------
At fixed kappa supplied by an already proved bound B, c=1/(6*kappa)
is the actual plain capacity coefficient, because two copies of the
plain witness require 2m+6*kappa*z<=1. Thus replacing a baseline capacity
by an inferior one, and its old Delta/4 penalty, is unnecessary.

The two short-count exponents are exactly
  A_I(r)=1-delta*[x+(1-x)r],
  S_t(r)=1-delta*[2cx+(2-4cx)(t-r)].
Their slopes have opposite signs and their crossing has denominator
D=3-(1+4c)x. For c in [2/9,3/13], x in [0,1/2],
  D>=53/26,
  r_*(1)>=(3-2c)/(5-4c)>=23/37,
  r_*(3/2)=1,
  1/3<=t-r_*(t)<=1/2.
These prove that every selected inverse capacity is <=7/37 and has
the same strict second-width margin 9/37 after the decrement. Every
selected plain capacity is <=c/3<=1/13. Supply >1/5 is therefore
more than sufficient. The source's zero-capacity and r=1 neighborhoods
continue to use the unrestricted zero-slot estimates.

With P=(2-4cx)(1-x), the crossing and long count balance at
  J=(5/6-delta)D+delta*P,
  t=1+delta*P/(2J),
  R=1-delta+(5/6-delta)*delta*P/(2J).
P>=10/13 and D>P make J uniformly positive on the closed parameter
rectangle. There is no singularity at delta=5/6; there t=3/2 and
R=1-delta, which is also provided by the source's no-slot endpoint
argument. With B=34999/40000 the actual bin ceiling is smaller than
5/6, so certifying the larger rectangle is conservative.

3. Low-side normalization and the changed geometry
--------------------------------------------------
The manuscript's fixed-geometry row-norm conclusion cannot simply be
copied when ell>1/6. The amendment correctly returns to the proof.
At M=1-ell, M'=M-2d and ell'=ell-d, the proof's key identity remains
  M'+ell'-1=-3d.
It gives T_d<=H-3d+o(1), and its two reflected-energy branches give
  E_ref<=M'+((1+3ell'-2M')/4)_++o(1)
       =M'+((5ell-1+d)/4)_++o(1).

After summing rescaled tuples the extra exponent is
  f(d)=-d+(5ell-1+d)_+/8.
For ell<=1/5 it is nonpositive on [0,ell]: it starts at zero and
has slopes -1 and -7/8. Thus the original total low estimate survives
despite the positive energy correction of some individual subsets.
The stage-one certificate checks ell<1/5 and the requisite positive
rescaled lengths and Gram domination with substantial margins.

Independently checking the normalization:
  lx=(1-ell-b)/2, ly=(1-ell+b)/2,
  h=1-lx+ell=(1+3ell+b)/2,
  C(s)=s-(4+b)/6.
The low exponent lx/2+b/12 equals C(theta) precisely at
theta=11/12-ell/4. This is exactly the certificate's ell definition.
An equality of these exponents is allowed: under beta_*>theta the
positive contradiction gap absorbs the arbitrarily small low-side
loss. No fixed positive low-side margin is required.

4. Euler regions and the contour/tail amendments
-----------------------------------------------
The four shared contour statements do print sigma0>=7/8. The amendment
must be included in the new theorem, rather than treating them as
literally applicable without change. I inspected their uses and the
source's local-factor proofs around lines 4080--4180 and 8655--9190.

At x>=theta, w>=19/20, z>=33/200, theta>=437/500:
the denominators 1-D, 1-V, 1-R stay uniformly nonzero;
the good-prime error powers 4-6x-6z and 1-x-w-6z remain below -1;
the other displayed defect powers, including -x-w, are below -1;
and the ramified defects remain strictly negative. Thus the required
positive majorants, holomorphy, and subpower bounds persist.

The principal approximation powers -x, -6z, 4-5x-6z, 1-w-6z
are all <=-theta. Hence the principal multiplier remains
  B_p=-1+O(q_p^(-theta)),
uniformly in phases and heights. The principal residue normalization
and a positive approximation margin survive after replacing 7/8
by theta in the relevant inequality.

On the small-row line x=beta_*+e, w=1/2, z=17/50, choose a first-region
epsilon0<=theta-1/2, rather than the hard-coded 3/8. The displayed
off-row powers remain negative. On a ramified row the strict term
has exponent <=1/2; the other boundary powers -1/2, 3/2-2x,
2-3x, 3-5x remain <=1/2. Thus the quotient-free local tuple bounds
used for small rows survive. Large-row estimates use the unchanged
absolute line (2,2,z_infinity).

5. Exact certificate and closure
-------------------------------
I reran:
  python3 research_audit/bootstrap_certificate.py
  python3 research_audit/bootstrap_certificate.py --negative-control
and saved an independent output copy as bootstrap_stage1_gram_referee.json.

The first stage uses
  B=34999/40000,
  kappa=14999/20000,
  theta=17499141/20000000=0.87495705,
  b=6170279/50000000,
  c=10000/44997.
Its Bernstein proof has 24 leaves and certifies E(h)<=-10^(-8) on
the entire rectangle delta in [0,5/6], x in [0,1/2]. The separate
floor, intermediate-row, small-row, supply, local and rescaling checks
pass. The negative control detects a negative point and rejects.

I checked the polynomial construction against the generalized source
formula
  E(d)=a-theta+h*(z0-1/6)-a*ly-(1-a)*ell
       -(delta/2-q)*ell+d*(R+delta/2-z0).
At d=h, q=x*delta, the constructed polynomial is exactly
J*(-E(h)-margin). The bivariate power-to-Bernstein conversion is the
standard exact identity b_ij=sum a_kl*binom(i,k)/binom(n,k)
*binom(j,l)/binom(m,l) after affine substitution to a unit box.
Nonnegative coefficients bound the polynomial on the whole rectangle;
this is not a sampled numerical test.

The final proof still must choose zeta>0 small enough to preserve
both supply and the endpoint margin above d=h. This is possible:
R>=1-delta gives a positive row slope, and R<=17/12 gives a uniform
upper slope below 2 on the stated rectangle. Thus taking, for example,
zeta<min(5ell-h,margin/8) suffices before allocating the other real
losses. The intermediate and floor slopes are also positive and
their certified endpoint margins are much larger.

All remaining choices have the same valid order as in the source:
choose small moment/rounding losses; choose their uniform mesh; choose
a fixed sufficiently large even number of disjoint slots; then choose
principal approximation, contour and amplitude losses; finally choose
target-dependent height ceilings after finite internal orders. Large
K or small real margins affect constants and eventual thresholds,
not the exponent inequalities.

6. Novelty and presentation caveats
----------------------------------
This first stage beats Liu's algebraic value 0.874957069799..., but
gram_split/LIT_ideas234.md already records a public Li Xiang claim
0.874957019420... described as a kappa-feedback cubic boundary. Its
primary source and proof must be checked before describing the present
mechanism or a further bootstrap as new research. This observation
does not invalidate the theorem implication audited above.

The appropriate theorem status is a proved analytic amendment plus an
exact arithmetic certificate, conditional only on accepting the upstream
source theorems and the fully written final contour/parameter closure.
It should not be advertised as a new Lean-verified theorem unless the
changed analytic lemma, not only the numerical certificate, is formally
proved in the actual dependency chain.

7. Final complete-note review and stage two
-----------------------------------------
I subsequently reviewed research_audit/kappa_bootstrap_proof.tex in full.
The explicit final closure now reserves zeta below both the supply and
endpoint margins, and puts the moment/mesh/slot/principal-margin choices
in their required order. In particular, the principal approximation
margin mu is chosen only after the slot count, and no earlier mesh
choice is required to depend on this later margin.

I independently reran the preliminary certificate.py: all checks pass,
with 35 boxes and endpoint margin 9/100000. I also independently ran
bootstrap_certificate.py --stage 2. It passes with 26 exact Bernstein
rectangles at theta=43747851/50000000=0.87495702, with kappa=7499141/10000000
justified by the preceding stage and endpoint margin 1/1000000000.

The new low-only 13/15 obstruction has an exact algebraic identity:
  M/12-ell/6+e/2-1/30
   = (1/5)[e-(1+3ell-2M)/4]
     +(1/60)[e-(M+ell-1)]+(17/60)e.
All three terms are nonnegative under the unchanged direct majorant's
listed hypotheses. Together with g>=b/6 this forces B>=13/15.
It is a lower bound for the supplied sufficient majorant, independent
of improvements in high-side row counts. It is not a lower bound for
actual zeros or an impossibility theorem for other arithmetic estimates.

My final assessment is that the note supplies a complete ordinary
mathematical proof by citation and modification of the original source
proofs, with exact arithmetic for its new finite inequalities. It is
not merely a framework contingent on an unproved new moment hypothesis.
This judgment imports the source's stated analytic results, as explicitly
declared, and does not independently certify all of their proofs. It also
does not amount to Lean kernel verification of this new bound. No LiXiang
ArithmeticProbeObligation is imported as an established theorem.
