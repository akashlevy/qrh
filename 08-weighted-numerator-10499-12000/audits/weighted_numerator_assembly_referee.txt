Verdict
-------
I found no material missing hypothesis or invalid inference in the
assembled implication

   original analytic package, including beta_*<=7/8
          => beta_*<=10499/12000.

The scope is primitive finite-order Hecke L-functions over
Q(sqrt(-3)), then all finite-order Hecke L-functions there and all
Dirichlet L-functions by the imported transfer theorem.  The conclusion
is NOT general GRH over arbitrary number fields, and is NOT an
end-to-end Lean theorem.  The manuscript states these limitations.

This verdict accepts the original analytic package as imported results.
It does not certify their internal correctness, the existing Lean proof
of 7/8, the external public benchmark's verification, or the entire new
proof in a proof assistant.  Exact exponent arithmetic establishes only
the inequalities to which the analytic estimates reduce the argument.

1. Low estimate and changed geometry
------------------------------------
The source reflected-energy lemma is stated for general bounded
log-lengths, not only ell=1/6.  Its concrete probe-row proof uses
M'+ell'-1=-3v to control the maximum involving the dual row length.
That identity remains exact here because M=1-ell.  The two branch
bounds before specialization become

  M'-Delta_H,
  M'+(1+3ell'-2M'-2Delta_H)/4,

up to the permitted small errors.  They are nonincreasing in Delta_H;
the actual residual-row condition Delta_H>=-o(1) thus gives the stated
M'+(5ell-1+v)_+/4 bound.  The argument retains the actual row support
and the kernel saving before any positive enlargement, as required by
the source lemma.  No optimistic replacement of an actual residual
row dyad by a larger ideal length is introduced.

The additive Gram theorem is stated for arbitrary polynomial scales
with P_a=Y'^2/Q>=1.  Here P_a is a fixed constant times Z^b and b>0.
Its nonexceptional term P_a^2/Y' is dominated by P_a^(1/6) because
ly-ell-11b/6=809/6000>0.  The smallest rescaled X and Y exponents are
413/2000 and 117/400 respectively; M'>0 throughout.

Tuple counting and the compensation coefficient give precisely -v;
the energy excess adds (5ell-1+v)_+/8.  Its maximum on [0,ell] is zero,
because ell<1/5 and both slopes are negative.  Thus the low estimate
uses the same physical probe and normalizer as the high estimate.
Its exponent equals C(B) for B=11/12-ell/4=10499/12000.

2. Row counts and their hypotheses
----------------------------------
The original fixed-parameter moment is available at kappa=3/4 by the
already established global beta_*<=7/8.  Nothing in the rederived row
count requires beta_*>7/8: that was the source application's setting,
but the actual estimates need kappa admissibility and delta<=alpha.
Both hold here, with delta<=3/4<5/6.  There is no capacity-replacement
loss at fixed kappa=3/4.

The rederived crossing parameter t lies in [1,3/2].  The source's
crossing estimates r_*(t)>=23/37 and t-r_*(t)>=1/3 therefore give
capacities at most 7/37 and 2/27.  Supply holds with
ell/h-7/37=1249/58719>0.  Positive whole slots, zero-amplitude slots,
small-capacity neighborhoods, and the original natural zero masks are
treated exactly as in the source selection argument.

The reconstructed numerator does not need to furnish another detector
witness.  The existing count is for the same amplitude/witness set;
the extra moment is a separate Hölder bound on that set.  Reusing the
same physical prime polynomials in these two valid bounds is legitimate
and does not require a joint inverse/plain moment.  They remain in the
physical correction throughout.

3. New numerator estimate and contour order
-------------------------------------------
For the new branch, delta>=1/3 and a>=2/3.  On w=1/2 and on the
coefficient-norm line w=1/2-delta/4, the first Euler region has the
uniform margin s_r+w_r-1>=delta/4>=1/12.  Thus all local identities
and all-height correction bounds apply with ample room.  The scalar
denominator remains buffered only in the s variable; no buffered
bound for the numerator at arbitrarily large w-height is used.

The source fixed-bin contour proof can be modified explicitly as follows:
  (a) Move z to17/50 and s to beta_*+20e on global contours.
  (b) Move w to1/2, keeping its full vertical line.
  (c) Discard only the s,z height tails while s remains global.
  (d) Move the retained s segment to a+16e.
  (e) Estimate its horizontal joins using the buffered reciprocal,
      the global polynomial numerator bound on the whole w axis, and
      the all-height correction bound.
  (f) Integrate w exactly before subdividing amplitudes at fixed s,z.

These are the same operations as the source proof with the harmless
replacement of its old w-line by1/2.  The source proof explicitly notes
that on an s-join the w and z axes may be extended completely because
the reciprocal depends only on s.  Thus completing the w integration
does not accidentally continue an amplitude indicator or move a
buffered reciprocal outside its valid height rectangle.

The Euler coefficient argument is stronger than a pointwise bound and
retains the cancellation G_p+bar(chi_p(u))H_p before taking absolute
coefficients.  The local formulas are polynomials in Np^(-w) after
their w-independent denominators are simplified.  Their products form
an absolutely convergent coefficient series on both required lines.
No arbitrary row-dependent coefficients are inserted into lem:plain:
the weighted l1 coefficient norm removes them before the moment.

For strict ramified errors, the distinct forced prime dilations are
absorbed once by C_u<=C U product p^{-(j_p-1)}.  Non-strict errors are
already dominated by their main-slot normalization.  The quotient-free
expansion excludes only error labels from the Euler product and leaves
every main-slot coefficient and zero mask unchanged.

The Mellin inversion formula is valid on w=1/2 by absolute coefficient
norm plus polynomial L-growth.  For each coefficient one can identify
the inverse Mellin transform on a right half-plane and shift back,
since the physical numerator is nonprincipal.  Compact support of W1
gives the exact k<=C Y cutoff before reflection.  Consequently reflected
plain lengths remain bounded, and the source scale-supremum/reflection
lemma applies without an unbounded-length moment assertion.

Natural good-prime conductors are exactly rad(u); only fixed S factors
remain to be restored.  Reflection changes chi to bar(chi), matching
the main slots; conjugating the entire product supplies the positive
orientation in the imported moment statement.  Moderate rows outside
the bounded unit range induce outside Theta because they ramify at a
good prime.  No exceptional family has been silently included in a
positive-slot moment.

For d in[1/2,h], ly<d<2ly, so n0=1-ly/d is positive and below1/2.
The capacity is supplied with base-Z margin
ell-(2ly-1/2)/(6kappa)=133/1800>0.  The dilation cost beats the capacity
loss by at least5delta/36.  Thus dyadic scales, annular decompositions,
and the small loss used to create capacity slack can all be paid for
without changing the ideal exponent.

5. Principal term and continuation quantifiers
----------------------------------------------
The new B remains well inside the explicit second Euler region after
the local exponents are checked.  Good-prime defects are summable
uniformly for Re s>=B; every ramified local defect still decays by a
fixed positive power.  The principal slot approximation is therefore
-1+O(p^{-B}).  Increasing the fixed prime cutoff makes the unmodified
principal correction H_eta uniformly within1/2 of1 on Re s>B.
Additional excluded target primes preserve this positive majorant.

The source residue-interface proof then applies with the same two
scalar poles, at w=1 and z=1/6, and no extra pole.  The principal
remainder margins are ly/20=919/40000 and h/600=529/400000, plus a
positive slot-approximation margin chosen after the slot system.
The principal target itself causes no problem: its reciprocal has a
zero at its pole, not an extra residue in the s move.

Under Delta=beta_*-B>0, the low exponent has marginDelta and the
nonprincipal main estimate has a fixed1/10000 margin even relative
to C(B).  All required real losses, the slot count, their minimum
length, the principal approximation power, and contour buffers can
therefore be chosen before the target.  Constants and finite seminorm
orders may depend on the target.  The late-height-closure lemma then
chooses the target-dependent height exponent and external tail order
without changing the common positive saving sigma.  The functions
J_eta and f_eta themselves are independent of the analysis height.

The continuation criterion requires precisely these quantifiers and
the uniform nonvanishing contraction for H_eta.  Choosing
omega=Delta/2 and a common remaining sigma>0 gives its contradiction
for a zero arbitrarily close to the family supremum; no zero is assumed
to attain that supremum.  Deleted finite Euler factors have no zeros
in Re s>0.  The imported norm-pullback transfer gives the Dirichlet
claim, with the usual principal-pole case handled by that theorem.
