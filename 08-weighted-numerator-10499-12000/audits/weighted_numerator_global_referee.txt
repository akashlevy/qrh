      O_eta,N(Z^B_eta*T1^(-N)).

8. QUANTIFIER ORDER, HEIGHTS, AND CONTINUATION

Under the contradiction Delta=beta_*-B>0, geometry, row extension,
finite exponent margins, small moment losses, capacity decrements, mesh,
slot count and fixed window shapes are chosen independently of the target.
The common mesh exists by the original uniform statement. Only after
slot lengths are fixed can one choose mu<B*min ell_i. Remaining detector
and contour losses can then be chosen below both the high margins and
mu, and the low loss can be chosen below Delta. The actual target then
supplies its finite arithmetic data, fixed cutoff and finite internal
profile/height orders. The positive height exponent may depend on that
target, while its product with those orders is made smaller than a fixed
reserved exponent. The external integration-by-parts order is chosen
last and raises only external test requirements, not an already fixed
internal height cost.

For maximal precision, choose the large-row absolute contour height v
immediately after the row extension zeta and before fixing internal
seminorm orders; v is a fixed real parameter in the resulting finite
range list. This is possible independently of the target. The manuscript's
reference to the unchanged large-row contour is consistent with this
choice but could state it explicitly.

All error estimates then have target-independent positive exponent
margins; constants and sufficiently large thresholds may depend on the
target. In particular, the high comparison may use the minimum of the
fixed endpoint/small-row margins, principal residue margins and mu,
after reserving losses. The low exponent satisfies omega<Delta. Both
functions J_eta and f_eta remain independent of the chosen analysis
height. They therefore satisfy the original continuation criterion with
sigma0=B and C(s)=s-(4+b)/6. That criterion is stated for arbitrary
sigma0 in (1/2,1), so its conclusion requires no threshold extension.

The claimed 13/15 barrier is an obstruction of the stated low inequalities
only. Its displayed positive linear combination is algebraically correct.
It does not purport to rule out an arithmetic improvement of those input
bounds or a different method reaching 1/2.
