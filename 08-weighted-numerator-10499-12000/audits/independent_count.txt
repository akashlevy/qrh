WEIGHTING THE RECONSTRUCTED PHYSICAL NUMERATOR
Research derivation, 2026-10-09.  This is a new analytic amendment to the
imported paper78.tex package, not a Lean verification and not an independent
verification of that package.  All statements below retain its physical
sixth-power-free rows and original zero extensions.

Main conclusion.
---------------
Fix kappa=3/4, using the already established beta_* <= 7/8.  For a retained
nonprincipal dynamic bin with a >= .51, delta=2a-1, row scale U=Z^d,
numerator scale Y=Z^ly, total physical slot length ell, amplitude q in
[0,delta/2], and a proved cardinality exponent R, the existing high bound
can be supplemented by

 E_W(d) = a-theta + h(z0-1/6) - ly/2 - ell/2 + q ell
          + d(3R/4+1/4-z0) - q(2ly-d)/(12 kappa),                 (W)

provided d/2 < ly < d and

       0 <= (2ly-d)/(6 kappa) <= ell.                            (S)

All statements have arbitrarily small exponent losses for mesh, smooth
scale boxes, and preliminary powers, and the fixed polynomial height
losses of the original package.  At endpoints in (S), one can use a small
capacity loss.  If the first quantity is negative, replace it by zero;
the formula as displayed is only claimed in the positive-capacity range.

This differs from merely applying the global fourth moment to L(1/2).
The w integral reconstructs an actual smooth sum of length Y.  Functional
equation reflection makes its length at most U/Y, leaving prime capacity
z_P=(1-2(1-ly/d))/(6 kappa).  The same physical prime factors can be used
inside this fourth moment, without removing them from the correction.
Hölder extracts half of their selected amplitude.  The Euler correction
and its error slots do not spoil this capacity: their long dilation tails
are paid for by absolute convergence and conductor deficits, as proved
below.  No new inverse or plain moment theorem is assumed.

The old exponent and (W) may be compared after choosing a whole dynamic
bin's contour strategy.  Without a further argument the safe operation is
min(max_q E_old, max_q E_W) at each fixed delta, not max_q min(E_old,E_W).
Amplitude sets are formed only after contour deformation; they are not
individually analytically continued.  The new argument integrates w
completely before making the pointwise amplitude partition in z.

2. The absolute coefficient norm of the correction.
---------------------------------------------------
Keep s and z fixed with Re s=a+16e and Re z=z0=17/50.  Set rho=delta/4,
and sigma_-=1/2-rho.  This is positive, and

     Re s + sigma_- - 1 >= delta/4 >= 1/200.

Thus the first Euler region holds on both Re w=1/2 and Re w=sigma_-.
All estimates in this section concern the sum of absolute coefficients
in the polynomial variable Np^(-w), not just its value at a particular
imaginary part.  This stronger claim follows from the displayed local
formulas by taking absolute values after their exact cancellations.
The denominators 1-D, 1-R and 1-V are independent of w and bounded away
from zero.  H_p is a polynomial of degree at most two in Np^(-w) at
p not dividing u, and at most one at p dividing u.

The identity eq:local-H-defect and Ecal_p=P_p^*+D give

    ||H_p-1||_{sigma_-} <= C Np^(-1-epsilon_H),  p not dividing u,
    ||H_p-1||_{sigma_-} <= C Np^(-epsilon_H),    p dividing u,

with epsilon_H>0 uniform on a in [.51,1] and the fixed buffer range.
Here ||sum b_j Np^(-jw)||_sigma = sum |b_j| Np^(-j sigma).
In particular any product excluding an arbitrary fixed tuple of slot
primes has absolute coefficient norm <= U^eps on both lines.  This is
the same convergent-prime-product/divisor-product argument as in the
original unselected-local-product estimate.  Exact simplification before
the coefficient norm is essential; a triangle bound on the uncancelled
P_p would not give this assertion.

For a subset I of error slots, use the quotient-free expansion

  F_I(u;s,w,z) = sum_{(p_i)_{i in I}}
       product_i [W_i(Np_i/P_i) Np_i^(z-1) E_{p_i}]
       product_{p not in S or {p_i}} H_p,

where the last product means p outside S and outside the selected tuple.
The other, main slots stay outside F_I as their actual polynomials
-P_i^(z-1/2)Q_i(u;z).  This identity follows by expanding each selected
local factor as -bar(chi_p(u))H_p + E_p.  It never divides by H_p.

At p not dividing u, old-eq:5.13b gives the coefficient-norm bound

 ||E_p||_sigma <= C( Np^(-s_r) + Np^(-6z0)
                     + Np^(4-5s_r-6z0)
                     + Np^(1-sigma-6z0) ).

For sigma=1/2 or sigma_-, every exponent is at most -1/2 with a fixed
margin (the closest is -a <= -.51).  Summing the O(P_i) primes in that
slot, including Np^(z-1), costs at most P_i^(z0-1/2).  Norm twists and
slot profiles have only their specified finite-seminorm/height costs.

At p dividing u, E_p=G_p and

     G_p = bar(eta(p)) Np^s (1-V) Ecal_p - Np^(-w) H_p.

After dividing its slot factor Np^(z-1) by Np^(z0-1/2), the non-tail
boundary terms from the local table have the following real exponents
at s_r=a and sigma=sigma_-:

  j=1: -1+rho;
  j=2: -delta;
  j=3: -delta+rho and -3delta/2;
  j=4: -3delta/2+rho;
  j=5: -5delta/2.

All are negative.  The R and V tails and -Np^(-w)H_p are smaller.
The one exception is the strict j>=2 term
  -eta(p)(Np-1) Np^(-s-w),
whose normalized slot factor costs at most Np^rho.  At sigma=1/2
it costs at most one.  Terms with positive e only improve these bounds.
There are divisor-many ramified labels.  For a fixed triangle-expanded
tuple let J be exactly its strict ramified labels.  They are distinct
because the physical prime supports are disjoint.  The conductor bound
gives

      (C_u/(C'_S U))^rho product_{p in J} Np^rho <= 1.

Expand F_I as sum_k A_{I,u}(k) Nk^(-w).  Absolute convergence and the
preceding bounds therefore prove simultaneously

  sum_k |A_{I,u}(k)| Nk^(-1/2) <= U^eps B_I,

  (C_u/(C'_S U))^rho
       sum_k |A_{I,u}(k)| Nk^(-1/2+rho) <= U^eps B_I,            (A)

where B_I=product_{i in I}P_i^(z0-1/2), up to harmless fixed profile
constants.  The second estimate applies the conductor inequality once
to the entire strict-label tuple.  It does not spend the same deficit
separately for different labels.  Combining the two gives

 sum_k |A_{I,u}(k)| Nk^(-1/2)
       max(1,C_u Nk/(C'_S U))^rho <= U^eps B_I.                (A')

This is the needed weighted coefficient assertion.  It is stronger than
an estimate |F_I(1/2+it)| <= U^eps B_I and is the reason w-Mellin
reconstruction remains valid with the actual correction.

3. Exact Mellin reconstruction and reflection.
-----------------------------------------------
For each fixed nonprincipal physical row move w from its starting line
to Re w=1/2, keeping the whole row's holomorphic correction.  No pole is
crossed: the physical numerator is nonprincipal and the Euler region
holds throughout.  W1hat has arbitrary decay.  The row set defining the
dynamic bin is independent of w.  One may then integrate the full w
line before estimating or making the amplitude partition in z.

Absolute convergence of the coefficient series on Re w=1/2 and the
usual polynomial strip growth justify termwise integration.  Mellin
inversion gives exactly

 (1/(2pi i)) int_(1/2) Y^(w-1) W1hat(w) L^S(w,chi_u) F_I(w) dw
   = Y^(-1/2) sum_k A_{I,u}(k) Nk^(-1/2)
                    S_{chi_u}(Y/Nk; W1).                    (M)

The plain sum S is centrally normalized.  Smooth reflection replaces
S_{chi_u}(X) by a unit root number times S_{bar chi_u}(C_u/X;W1sharp),
with the original natural zeros restored exactly as in the source.
All redundant factors here lie at fixed S.  Their deletion/restoration
has bounded weighted coefficient mass for rho<1/2, and their fixed
scale factors are absorbed into C'_S.  In particular, no new moving
radical or new row mask is created.  Annular decomposition and scale
boxes, as in the source reflection proof, give arbitrarily small
length losses and polynomial height/seminorm losses.

For T=max(1,C_u Nk/(C'_S U)), (A') bounds (M) by a weighted rowwise
supremum of reflected sums of scales at most C'_S (U/Y)T, with weight
T^(-rho), times Y^(-1/2) U^eps B_I.  The profile is fixed; only its
scale is row-dependent.  Thus the smooth-calculus scale-supremum lemma
applies directly.  The original W1 is compactly supported, say in [r_-,r_+].
Before reflection, (M) is identically zero for Nk>r_+Y, since every
nonzero ideal has norm at least one.  Thus the coefficient sum relevant
to (M) may be truncated exactly at Nk<=r_+Y.  Consequently T is in a
fixed polynomial range and every reflected scale is at most a fixed
multiple of U.  The source's smooth scale-supremum and bounded-length
moment assertions therefore apply directly.  Reflected-profile upper
annuli are handled by its ordinary arbitrarily decaying annular tail;
no moment with unbounded declared length is needed.

4. Weighted fourth moment and the dilation tails.
-------------------------------------------------
Fix a pointwise amplitude set C at one retained tuple (s,z) and its
original witness subdivision, with #C <= U^(R+eps).  The cardinality
bound was established by the original detector; no new large-value
witness for the reconstructed numerator is needed.  Write

 L=ell/d, n0=1-ly/d, z_P=(1-2n0)/(6 kappa).

For T=U^v let n(v)=n0+v, and

     z(v)=max(0,z_P-2v/(6 kappa)).

Condition (S) ensures z_P<=L.  Choose the largest-amplitude main slots
up to this total length, with mesh loss.  If there are too few main
slots, select all of them; the remaining physical slots have assigned
amplitude zero.  The selection lemma then gives

     |Q_selected| >= U^(q z(v)-O(mesh))

on C, while |Q_all| <= U^(qL+O(mesh)).  Every actual selected length
is no larger than z(v).  The reflected numerator and these slots both
use bar(chi_p(u)); the fixed phases and natural zero extensions match.
Conjugating the whole product within its absolute value gives the
positive row orientation in the imported plain-moment statement, while
only conjugating the profiles and fixed finite-ray coefficients.  For
large U in the moderate dyads, every physical row has a good ramified
prime, so its inducing character is outside the fixed group Theta.
Unit and fixed-prime-supported exceptions belong to the small-row bound.
No selected prime is removed from Q_all.

Hölder and lem:plain yield

 sum_{u in C} |S_{bar chi_u}(n(v)) Q_all(u)|
   <= U^(qL-qz(v)/2+eps) (#C)^(3/4)
         (sum_u |S_{bar chi_u}(n(v))|^4 |Q_selected(u)|^2)^(1/4)
   <= U^(qL-qz(v)/2+3R/4+1/4+eps).                         (H)

For z(v)>0 its capacity condition is 2n(v)+6 kappa z(v)=1.
For z(v)=0 the source's zero-slot assertion has no restriction on the
bounded nonnegative plain length.  Smooth derivatives and supremum
boxes are taken before this application, exactly as in the source.

The coefficient weight T^(-rho) adds -rho v.  Until the capacity is
exhausted, the exponent relative to v=0 is

            (q/(6 kappa)-rho)v.

Since kappa=3/4, q<=delta/2, and rho=delta/4,

       rho-q/(6 kappa) >= delta(1/4-1/9)=5delta/36>0.

After the capacity is exhausted the exponent continues decreasing
with slope -rho.  Thus all dilation dyads are bounded by the v=0
exponent, with room for the mesh, annular, and smooth supremum losses.
The same argument also treats the actual conductor C_u smaller than U:
it only reduces the reflected length, and was already accounted for
in (A').  All error subsets I satisfy the same estimate.

Combining (M), (H), and the normalization of the main slots gives the
whole w-integrated bin contribution, before the other Mellin weights,

      Y^(-1/2) Z^(ell(z0-1/2)+q ell)
          U^(3R/4+1/4 - qz_P/2 + eps).

The factor U^(-z0), reciprocal denominator bound U^eps, and remaining
X,Z Mellin weights are exactly those in the original high-bin proof.
Their combination is (W).  All amplitude partitions are made at this
point and are used only to bound the already continued full bin.

5. Endpoint in the row scale and compatibility with the old bound.
-----------------------------------------------------------------
Within the positive-capacity range, the coefficient of d in (W) is

          3R/4+1/4-z0 + q/(12 kappa).

Since R>=1-delta and delta<=5/6, it is at least
          3(1/6)/4+1/4-.34 = .035>0.
Thus the largest moderate row scale d=h controls (W), just as it does
the original high exponent.  The physical small-row and far-row
estimates can be retained from the original proof.  Any use at a
different range of d must check (S); no capacity is silently requested
when its right side is negative or exceeds the physical supply.

The difference from the old high exponent at fixed R,q is

 E_W-E_old = delta ly/2
             + d[(1-R)/4-delta/2] - q(2ly-d)/(12 kappa).

At q=delta/2, d=h, R=2/3 and M=lx+ly=1-ell, this becomes

       h/12-delta ell-delta(2ly-h)/(24 kappa).

The last term is the additional saving beyond vanilla central L4.
It is obtained from a physical plain sum already present in the probe,
not from a stronger zero-free premise or a feedback optimization.

Earlier avenues that did not yield an improvement:
-------------------------------------------------
1. Replacing global kappa in the plain moment by the physical parent
   zero-bin delta is invalid: Poisson-transformed child rows need not
   inherit that bin.  Keeping an arbitrary bin mask destroys the
   complete Gauss transforms used in the proof.  A new conditional
   child-row moment would be required.
2. Hölder combinations of already proved inverse and plain moments
   alone yield convex combinations of their savings.  Reusing prime
   slots in both moments without a genuine mixed moment is not free.
3. Applying only the unweighted global fourth moment to L(1/2) changes
   E_old by delta ly/2+d[(1-R)/4-delta/2].  Numerical optimization of
   that bound alone did not beat the public feedback limit.  The
   weighted reconstructed numerator supplies the extra term in (W).
4. One can factor the leading row-independent Euler term
   D W=eta(p)Np^(-s-w) to extract L_F^S(s+w,eta), extending the Euler
   region across s+w=1.  For principal eta its residue simplifies by
   the numerator functional equation.  This is a separate possible
   lever, but no improvement from it is asserted here and it is not
   needed in the weighted reconstruction argument.
