Independent low-side investigations, 9 October 2026

Task and result
---------------
Seek an improvement beyond the public benchmark 0.874957019420098946 by
changing the low-side probe, its projection, or combinations of its scales.
I used the original local manuscript and existing Gram-split calculations,
not Li Xiang's construction. No new positive power saving has been proved.
The following are precise failures or scoped obstructions, rather than a
claim that all future low-side approaches are impossible.

The source is formalize_11_12/papers/paper78.tex. Important labels are
general-probe, low-separated, probe-poisson-identity, reflection-row-phase,
signal, probe-gram, and the proof of probe-row-norm.

1. A promising-looking arithmetic twist fails for an exact reason
----------------------------------------------------------------
Candidate: replace the auxiliary residue character xi by one whose order
does not divide six. A sextic Kubota series twisted by such a character
might have its theta residue removed, potentially removing the Gram loss
without estimating the difficult phased pairing.

The original source explicitly chooses xi^6=1 at lines 3350--3355.
This is not a convenience that can be removed while keeping the same
high-side signal. The exact Poisson derivation at lines 3944--3946 uses

  conjugate(xi(H/s)) = conjugate(xi(u)) xi(s),

because the nonzero frequency is written H=u a^6 and xi(a)^6=1.
For a general xi the correct factor has the additional term

  conjugate(xi(a))^6.

Thus, after the same sixth-power completion, the scalar factor
zeta_F^S(6z) is replaced by

  L_F^S(6z, conjugate(xi)^6).

If the induced finite-order Hecke character conjugate(xi)^6 is
nonprincipal, it has no pole at 6z=1. The z=1/6 residue that creates
the principal signal is absent. The remaining fixed finite Euler
corrections cannot restore that pole. This is an exact obstruction
within this auxiliary-character modification, even if one grants its
desired cancellation on the Gram side. No hypothetical theta functional
equation is needed to identify the missing high-side pole.

It is important to phrase this in terms of the induced character, not
just the raw residue character's order: a component living entirely on
the units need not remain nonprincipal on the normalized ideal variables.
The conclusion applies precisely when xi^6 induces a nonprincipal
character on the good primary ideals.

2. The weight-cancellation obstruction extends to varying Q
----------------------------------------------------------
The earlier note states its obstruction at a fixed Q. The following
calculation removes that restriction for the same fixed arithmetic data,
same completed-row scale Z, and linear combinations of base probes.

Suppose the theta decomposition is available, with common rho_sigma(m)
and B_{m,sigma}(Z). For probe j let

  Q_j=q_b X_j Y_j,
  K_j(x)=integral W_{1,j}(t)t^(1/6)W_{0,j}(x/t) dt/t.

Combining the central Mellin integral gives its theta contribution

  I_theta,j = Q_j^(-1/2) Y_j^(-1/3)
      sum_{sigma,m} xi(m) rho_sigma(m) B_{m,sigma}(Z) K_j(Nm/Q_j).

The auxiliary majorant Omega can be omitted here because it is one
on the support of the Mellin convolution K_j. Fixed normalization
constants are common and do not affect the calculation.

For arbitrary coefficients a_j (including ones depending on Z), define

  H_Z(r)=sum_j a_j Q_j^(-1/2)Y_j^(-1/3) K_j(r/Q_j).

The combined theta contribution uses exactly this one radial kernel.
Its Mellin moment is

  integral H_Z(r) r^(5/6) dr/r
    = sum_j a_j Q_j^(1/3)Y_j^(-1/3) Khat_j(5/6)
    = q_b^(1/3) sum_j a_j X_j^(1/3)
                              W1hat_j(1) W0hat_j(5/6).

The final sum is precisely the combined principal-signal coefficient,
up to the common nonzero source normalization. Therefore

  H_Z identically zero  ==>  combined principal signal identically zero.

This allows different Q_j, different Y_j, different radial test weights,
and arbitrary finite coefficients. It is a stronger scope than the
fixed-Q cancellation statement in the old note. Its proof is only Mellin
convolution and a change of variable; it does not assume cancellation of
the difficult pi-pairing.

What this does NOT rule out: cancellation only after summing against the
actual arithmetic rho B; kernels tailored to a specific finite row set;
arithmetic changes of the probe; or different completed-row scales Z_j.
Those operations require new identities or correlation estimates. The
identity above rules out a universal radial-kernel annihilation as a
signal-preserving way to remove the theta component.

3. An exponent obstruction for sums of separately bounded probes
---------------------------------------------------------------
Lemma (finite/subpower superposition with triangle bounds).
Suppose constituent j has a supplied low bound Z^(L_j+o(1)) and a
zero-detecting signal of exponent beta+c_j. Multiply it by a coefficient
of size Z^(p_j+o(1)). For a fixed number of terms, or a uniformly
subpower number with corresponding uniform bounds, estimate the sum by
the triangle inequality or by Cauchy--Schwarz over the constituent index.
Assume the surviving combined signal has exponent beta+c_*.
Then the resulting zero-free threshold cannot be lower than
min_j(L_j-c_j).

Proof. The supplied low exponent is max_j(p_j+L_j). The signal exponent
satisfies c_*<=max_j(p_j+c_j), including the possibility of exact
leading-signal cancellations. Choose a j attaining the latter maximum.
Then

  max_i(p_i+L_i)-c_*
    >= (L_j-c_j)+(p_j+c_j-c_*)
    >= min_i(L_i-c_i).

A subpower number of terms changes neither maximum at the power level.
Cauchy--Schwarz in that index has the same maximal exponent. QED.

Consequently, taking several individually certified geometries does not
create a better exponent through averaging their existing bounds. One
must prove a cross-correlation or a cancellation estimate for the sum
itself. This is not a prohibition on such an estimate.

The same simple obstruction holds at the exponent-model level for
products with positive integer powers: if their signal and low exponents
add, the resulting boundary is a weighted average of constituent
boundaries. This latter observation assumes that the product is a valid
detector; it is not itself a construction of a nonlinear detector.

4. Orthogonal projection is a reformulation, not an estimate
-----------------------------------------------------------
At one fixed row block, write A=a rho+R and use the Hermitian pairing
so the physical bilinear sum is <A, conjugate(B)>. Let P be the
orthogonal projection onto rho. Then

  <(1-P)A, conjugate(B)> = <(1-P)R, conjugate(B)>.

The norm of this expression can indeed be estimated by the clean
residual bound. Equivalently one may project conjugate(B) instead.
However the physical probe has changed by exactly

  <P A, conjugate(B)>.

Writing down P does not estimate this subtracted arithmetic quantity or
supply a new high-side identity. The corrected reflection formula makes
its leading term involve pi(m)=rho(m) zeta_m, with

  zeta_m=(fixed ray factor) mu(m) conjugate(alpha(m))
                                      conjugate(gamma_2(m)).

Thus importing the old high-side signal after projection, without
accounting for this subtracted term, assumes the missing conclusion.
The elementary projection is well-defined; the proposed proof shortcut
is not established. This failure is distinct from the universal-kernel
annihilation theorem in section 2.

As a scale check, at the previously audited rational geometry
theta=0.87495702, b=24681137/200000000, h=324784289/400000000,
the clean residual threshold is

  1-h/6 = 2075215711/2400000000 = 0.86467321291666...,

whereas the retained theta Cauchy--Schwarz term increases it by
b/12=0.01028380708333.... The residual is therefore already smaller
by a fixed power. Improving or even discarding it alone cannot improve
the overall exponent while the theta pairing keeps its old estimate.

5. Angular modulation: a candidate, not a completed replacement
---------------------------------------------------------------
Another possible modification is a nonradial smooth weight in the m
variable, intended to cancel the angular part of zeta_m. This does not
by itself help the Gram norm, since multiplying rows by a unimodular
angular phase leaves that norm unchanged. It can change the arithmetic
pairing, which is the potentially useful feature.

But Fourier transformation preserves the angular harmonic while moving
its scale factors onto the dual frequency and the other arithmetic
variables. In the source Poisson setup this would put additional angular
characters on the s and completed-index sums. Consequently the w=1
principal numerator pole and the finite-order target character cannot
be assumed unchanged. Compensating those characters would in turn change
the completed theta row and its reflection law.

I have not derived the full nonradial high identity and the matching
reflected row. Therefore this is not an unconditional replacement lemma
and no improved exponent follows from it here. In particular the formal
identity alpha(m)^2 conjugate(gamma_2(m))^4=gamma_2(m)^2 for squarefree
m is insufficient: it concerns the desired row phase, not the rest of
the modified physical probe.

6. Precise current status
------------------------
Proved scoped obstructions: section 1's missing sixth-power-completion
pole; section 2's varying-Q kernel cancellation identity; section 3's
superposition exponent lemma.

Failed shortcut: projecting away the theta vector and reusing the old
high-side identity without estimating the removed pairing.

Unfinished candidate: a fully nonradial probe with compensated angular
characters. It needs a newly derived high identity and reflection law;
no saving is asserted.

No new theorem beating the public benchmark was obtained in this
low-side investigation. These results do not prove an unrestricted
optimality theorem for the method. They identify exactly which proposed
algebraic operations fail to create a power saving and where genuine
new arithmetic information would still be required.

7. Can rho cancel the reflected Gauss phase and expose Mobius?
------------------------------------------------------------
This is a more promising question than treating pi as an arbitrary
sequence, but its first algebraic simplification does not evaluate pi.

Primary source checked directly: Bröker--Hoffstein, Fourier coefficients
of sextic theta series, https://arxiv.org/html/1312.0568v1, equations in
the introduction immediately following the Hecke relation. Its stated
normalization gives tau_6(p)=G_2(1,p) tau_6(p^3). Their introduction
explicitly suppresses bad-prime and reciprocity details, so transporting
the following scalar formula to every cusp of our rho vector requires
those finite local conventions. The discussion below does not silently
claim that transport has been proved.

In compatible good-primary conventions, the local Hecke relation is

  tau_6(a p)=G_2(a,p) tau_6(a p^3),  (a,p)=1,
  G_2(a,p)=chi_p(a)^(-2) gamma_2(p).

Let m=p_1...p_k be squarefree. Apply the relation successively at p_i,
turning its exponent from 1 to 3. At step i, the other prime factors
already transformed to exponent 3 contribute trivially to G_2, whereas
the later primes contribute chi_(p_i)(p_j)^(-2). Therefore

  tau_6(m) = [product_i gamma_2(p_i)]
             [product_(i<j) chi_(p_i)(p_j)^(-2)] tau_6(m^3).

Cubic reciprocity identifies the displayed bracket with gamma_2(m):
gamma_2's twisted multiplicativity contributes
chi_(p_i)(p_j)^2 chi_(p_j)(p_i)^2
 = chi_(p_i)(p_j)^4 = chi_(p_i)(p_j)^(-2).
Finite ray corrections can be carried as a class vector if the primary
conventions have not made this identity scalar.

Using tau_6(m)=Nm^(1/12) rho(m), the idealized scalar identity is

  rho(m) conjugate(gamma_2(m)) = Nm^(-1/12) tau_6(m^3),
  pi(m) = (ray factor) mu(m) conjugate(alpha(m))
                              Nm^(-1/12) tau_6(m^3).

Equivalently pi(m)=(ray factor) mu(m) conjugate(alpha(m))
Nm^(1/6) rho(m^3). Thus there IS a formal cancellation of one cubic
Gauss factor. The remaining coefficient tau_6(m^3) is not thereby
computed, nor is it known from these relations to be multiplicative or
constant. The inverse local relation is tau_6(a p^3)=G_4(a,p)tau_6(a p),
with G_2(a,p)G_4(a,p)=1. It just inverts the same transport.

This limitation can be seen algebraically without invoking numerical
unpredictability: the good-prime exponent involution j -> 4-j modulo
the period six has orbit {1,3}. Neither exponent in this orbit is
connected to exponent 0 by the local Hecke relations. Consequently the
relations leave a free base coefficient for each squarefree index;
replacing exponents 1 by 3 does not reduce the number of free data.
Other analytic or global identities might constrain these data. The
local relations alone do not.

The blanket statement in some old notes that using row mu(m) would
automatically be circular is too strong. This is a different variable
from the detector's completed-index Mobius factor. A proved estimate
using its sign changes would be legitimate. The missing ingredient is
an estimate for the remaining theta coefficient, not a logical ban on
Mobius cancellation.

8. Exact power cost of a direct reciprocal-L strategy
----------------------------------------------------
Even grant the substantially stronger fictitious simplification

  pi(m) = mu(m) omega(m) Nm^(-1/12),

with omega a finite-order Hecke character, and retain the actual
quadratic kernel and column normalization. Suppose that zero-freeness
and reciprocal bounds give, for every finite-order chi of polynomial
conductor and every fixed smooth dyadic W,

  sum_m mu(m)chi(m)W(Nm/Q) << Q^(B+epsilon).

This implication follows by Mellin inversion from the conductor-uniform
subpower bound for 1/L on Re s>B, including the standard deleted Euler
factors. In the model above it yields, for each column n,

  F(n) = sum_m pi(m)(n/m)_2 W(Nm/Q)
       << Q^(B-1/12+epsilon).

The actual reflected column has sum_(n~L)|beta_n|^2 << Q^epsilon.
Applying the per-column estimate followed by Cauchy--Schwarz in n gives

  |T| <= sum_n |beta_n F(n)|
      << Q^(B-1/12+epsilon) L^(1/2).

Write L=Q^lambda. This beats the existing Q^(11/12+epsilon)
estimate precisely if

  lambda < 2(1-B).

At B=0.874957019420098946 the right side is
0.250085961159802108. At the relevant unmarked geometry M about 5/6,
Q=Z^M and L=Q^2/Z give lambda=2-1/M about 4/5. Marked rows may have
L about Q. Thus this direct strategy does not give a better exponent
even under the fictitious removal of the unknown theta coefficient.

Scope: this is an obstruction to the displayed per-column bound plus
absolute summation/Cauchy--Schwarz in columns, not to all averaged
Mobius estimates. For example, a new mean-square saving simultaneously
using beta's phases and the actual tau_6(m^3) sequence could evade it.

The real arithmetic has two further unresolved inputs: tau_6(m^3)
cannot be dropped, and conjugate(alpha(m)) is an infinite-order angular
Hecke factor. The source logarithmic-control lemma at paper78.tex
lines 1532--1552 concerns finite-order Hecke characters. Its global
zero-free conclusion therefore cannot simply be quoted for this
angular twist. No unrestricted impossibility is inferred from either
observation.
